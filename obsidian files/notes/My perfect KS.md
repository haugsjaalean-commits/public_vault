## The philosophy of a rhizome (according to me)


We have thoughts and minds. In the metaphor I'm about to make, it will help us to think of our minds as rhizome-like things. We see and feel things all the time, and we like it when we understand these things. Unfortunately, depending on who you ask, there is actually no way of ever understanding the things we experience every day. Despite this, our minds create paths and opinions--that's what they're for. Sometimes these paths work in our favor, and sometimes they don't, but we can always create new paths. A rhizome is not fixed to any given state; they can change, evolve, and produce new connexions, and so can we!

## Representing KUs




This KS is based on the creation of notes. Each of these notes represents a KU. The question is how to represent these KUs. I could say that there are two main design philosophies when it comes to defining the KUs using notes (`.md` files). Both methods assume that KCs are being represented by a table in a database; however, the difference is in how CCs are represented. The first approach assumes that CCs are simply defined by the contents of the note, and the second approach takes a wider view of the situation and bases the CCs on the greater concept of the note. 






The first approach represents the KUs in a logical manner by stating that the contents of each file represent its CC. 







## About knowledge systems







When creating knowledge systems, I believe that there is a certain way in which we need to be thinking about the KUs. Previously, I have considered that the KUs represent real-world things or concepts; however, I now believe this line of thinking to be flawed. The fact of the matter is that we are not organizing real-world things. We are organizing notes about real-world things. This is a key distinction, and will save us a lot of truble in the long run if we take the time to understand it. 

If we think about our KUs as representing the *things* themselves, then we risk forgetting what we are trying to organize is actually notes and note *things*. For this reason, I think that the best approach is to consider that the *core concepts* of the KUs are the very fact that they are a note which represents a thing.
